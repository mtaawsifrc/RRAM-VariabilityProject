\title{Physics-Guided, Identifiability-Aware Extraction of Stanford RRAM
Compact-Model Parameters from Experimental TaO$_x$ Devices: Methodology}

\author{...}% authors omitted in this methodology draft
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This document specifies, at implementation level, the proposed methodology for
extracting Stanford--PKU RRAM compact-model parameters from measured DC
$I$--$V$ characteristics of TaO$_x$ devices. The method (i) automatically
classifies the dominant conduction mechanism (ohmic, Poole--Frenkel, Schottky,
Fowler--Nordheim) per voltage window and resistance state, (ii) converts the
classified regimes into physics-informed priors and a mechanism-aware fitting
domain, (iii) fits the compact model by Bayesian optimization of full HSPICE
transient simulations followed by local refinement, and (iv) reports which
parameters are actually identifiable from DC data using a Fisher-information
analysis at the optimum, one-dimensional profile likelihoods, and cluster
bootstrap confidence intervals across measurement cycles.
\end{abstract}

% =========================================================================
\section{Methodology}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Overview and Rationale}
\label{sec:overview}

The goal of the proposed framework is \emph{not} merely to reproduce a
measured $I$--$V$ curve with the Stanford--PKU compact model
\cite{guan2012spice,jiang2014stanford}, but to answer a stricter question:
\emph{which compact-model parameters can be defensibly extracted from DC
$I$--$V$ data of a given device, and with what uncertainty?} Three
observations motivate the design.

First, the dominant conduction mechanism in TaO$_x$ RRAM is not unique: our
previous atomic-scale characterization \cite{chowdhury2025mwscas} showed that,
depending on oxygen stoichiometry, resistance state, and voltage window, the
high-resistance state (HRS) and low-resistance state (LRS) may be governed by
ohmic, Poole--Frenkel (PF), Schottky, or Fowler--Nordheim (FN) transport.
The Stanford model, however, expresses conduction through a single
bulk hopping/tunneling law (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}). Fitting that law to voltage
windows dominated by interface-limited (Schottky) or tunneling (FN) transport
silently corrupts the extracted parameters. We therefore classify the
conduction regimes \emph{first} and use them to (a) place the prior-extraction
windows, (b) weight the fitting loss toward the model's physical domain of
validity, and (c) interpret identifiability per mechanism.

Second, the Stanford model is over-parameterized with respect to a single DC
butterfly curve: several parameters (e.g., thermal resistance, characteristic
field) influence the simulated current only weakly or in compensating
combinations. Reporting a single best-fit vector without an identifiability
analysis invites over-interpretation. We therefore make identifiability an
\emph{operational} output: a Cram\'er--Rao analysis at the fitted optimum,
confirmed by profile likelihoods, classifies each parameter as identifiable,
weakly identifiable, or non-identifiable, and non-identifiable parameters are
reported at their prior values rather than claimed as extracted.

Third, RRAM exhibits strong cycle-to-cycle (C2C) variability; a fit to one
cycle is not a device characterization. We fit a measured \emph{medoid} cycle
(not a synthetic average) for the point estimate, and quantify how C2C
variability propagates into the parameters with a cluster bootstrap over whole
cycles and leave-one-cycle-out (LOCO) cross-validation.

Fig.~\ref{fig:flowchart} shows the complete pipeline;
Algorithms~\ref{alg:regimes} and \ref{alg:fit} give the two core procedures.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Device Fabrication and Electrical Measurement}
\label{sec:devices}

Ti/Pt/TaO$_x$/Ta/Pt cross-point devices ($1<x<2.5$) were fabricated as
reported in \cite{chowdhury2025mwscas,moazzeni2023}; the TaO$_x$ switching
layer was deposited by reactive DC sputtering, with oxygen stoichiometry
controlled through the O$_2$ partial pressure during deposition.
Twelve deposition/measurement conditions (S1--S12) are analyzed.

DC resistive-switching characteristics were measured with a Keysight B1500A
semiconductor parameter analyzer. Each measurement record contains bipolar
sweeps (SET: $0\!\to\!V_{\max}\!\to\!0$; RESET: $0\!\to\!V_{\min}\!\to\!0$)
with SMU current compliance ($I_{\mathrm{cc}}\approx\SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere}$
for the devices reported here) applied during electroforming and SET to
prevent hard breakdown. Per condition, tens of consecutive cycles were
recorded to expose C2C variability.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 0: Raw-Data Extraction and Cycle Quality Screening}
\label{sec:extraction}

Raw B1500 CSV exports are parsed into per-cycle voltage--current point sets,
preserving the instrument's block structure (SET/RESET labels). Branch
polarity is taken from the block label when present and inferred from voltage
polarity otherwise. A cycle is retained only if it passes all of the following
screens, chosen to reject open/shorted devices, aborted sweeps, and noise-floor
artifacts:

\begin{itemize}
  \item at least 40 measured points and voltage span $\ge\SI{1.0}{\volt}$;
  \item peak current between \SI{1}{\nano\ampere} and \SI{1}{\ampere};
        currents below \SI{0.1}{\pico\ampere} are treated as noise floor;
  \item dynamic range of $|I|$ of at least 0.5 decades;
  \item both SET and RESET content present.
\end{itemize}

Rejected cycles never contribute to any later stage. Each surviving point is
tagged with condition, device, cycle index, branch, and sweep position
(\texttt{point\_index}), which later preserves the hysteresis ordering.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 1: Medoid Representative Cycle}
\label{sec:medoid}

For each condition, the device with the strongest usable-cycle statistics is
selected, and a single measured cycle is chosen to represent it. All good
cycles of the device are interpolated in $\log_{10}|I|$ onto a common
branch-specific voltage grid; at each grid voltage the median log-current
defines the \emph{typical} curve. Each cycle $c$ is scored by its median
absolute deviation from the typical curve,
\begin{equation}
  s_c=\operatorname{median}_v\bigl|\log_{10}|I_c(v)|-
  \operatorname{median}_{c'}\log_{10}|I_{c'}(v)|\bigr|,
\end{equation}
and the cycle minimizing the joint SET/RESET rank is selected (the
\emph{medoid}). Unlike a pointwise median curve, the medoid is a real measured
sweep: it preserves kink structure, compliance behavior, and the exact
hysteresis sequence, and it gives later stages a defensible physical object to
fit. The representative CSV carries, per point, the branch, voltage, current
statistics across cycles (median and interquartile range, used later as noise
weights), and a butterfly sequence index.

In parallel, a \emph{per-cycle ensemble} file is built containing every good
cycle of the same device in the identical schema; this drives the bootstrap
and LOCO analyses of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty}.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 2: Automatic Conduction-Regime Classification}
\label{sec:regimes}

This stage automates the conduction-mechanism analysis of
\cite{chowdhury2025mwscas} and produces the \emph{regime map} used throughout
the rest of the pipeline.

\subsubsection{State segmentation}
The representative butterfly cycle is split into four state segments by the
voltage extrema along the measured sequence: \textsc{set-hrs-pre} (forward SET
sweep, device in HRS), \textsc{set-lrs-post} (SET return sweep, LRS),
\textsc{reset-lrs-pre} (forward RESET sweep, LRS), and \textsc{reset-hrs-post}
(RESET return sweep, HRS). Segmenting by sequence rather than by voltage is
essential: forward and return half-sweeps overlap in voltage but correspond to
different resistance states.

\subsubsection{Candidate mechanisms}
On each window $W$ of a segment (window construction below), the four standard
transport linearizations \cite{chiu2014} are regressed:
\begin{align}
  \text{ohmic:}& & \ln I &= m\ln V + b, & m&\in[0.8,1.2],
  \label{eq:ohmic}\\[2pt]
  \text{PF:}& & \ln (I/V) &= s_{\mathrm{PF}}\sqrt{V} + b, & s_{\mathrm{PF}}&>0,
  \label{eq:pf}\\[2pt]
  \text{Schottky:}& & \ln I &= s_{\mathrm{S}}\sqrt{V} + b, & s_{\mathrm{S}}&>0,
  \label{eq:schottky}\\[2pt]
  \text{FN:}& & \ln (I/V^{2}) &= s_{\mathrm{FN}}/V + b, & s_{\mathrm{FN}}&<0.
  \label{eq:fn}
\end{align}
Here $V$ denotes $|V|$ and $I$ denotes $|I|$ within the segment; the field is
$E\simeq V/t_{\mathrm{ox}}$ with oxide thickness
$t_{\mathrm{ox}}=\SI{5}{\nano\meter}$.

\subsubsection{Physical admissibility: dynamic permittivity}
The PF and Schottky linearizations, Eqs.~(\ref{eq:pf})--(\ref{eq:schottky}),
are nearly collinear on narrow windows, so a fit-quality criterion alone
cannot separate them. We therefore enforce the physical consistency check used
in the dielectric-conduction literature \cite{chiu2014}: the theoretical
slopes are
\begin{align}
  s_{\mathrm{PF}} &= \frac{q}{kT}\sqrt{\frac{q}{\pi\varepsilon_0
      \varepsilon_r t_{\mathrm{ox}}}}, &
  s_{\mathrm{S}} &= \frac{q}{kT}\sqrt{\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0
      \varepsilon_r t_{\mathrm{ox}}}},
\end{align}
which we invert to obtain the dynamic relative permittivity implied by the
fitted slope $s$:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}
  = \frac{q^{3}}{\pi\,\varepsilon_0\, t_{\mathrm{ox}}\,(s\,kT)^{2}}
  \times
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \text{PF},\\[2pt]
    \tfrac14, & \text{Schottky}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:epsdyn}
\end{equation}
A PF or Schottky candidate is admitted only if
$\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}\in[1,60]$, a deliberately generous bracket
around the optical ($n^2\!\approx\!4.8$) and static
($\varepsilon_r\!\approx\!25$) permittivities of TaO$_x$; the fitted
$\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is reported in the regime map so reviewers can
audit each assignment. The ohmic candidate is admitted only if its log--log
slope lies in $[0.8, 1.2]$, and FN only with negative slope. This is what
makes the classification a \emph{physics} test rather than a curve-shape
contest.

\subsubsection{Window construction and model selection}
Within each state segment, points are cleaned ($|V|\ge\SI{0.05}{\volt}$,
$|I|\ge\SI{0.1}{\pico\ampere}$) and the SMU compliance plateau is excised by
discarding points with $|I|\ge 0.95\,\max_W |I|$, since a current pinned at
the compliance limit carries no transport information. Sliding windows of
length $\max(8,\lceil N/3\rceil)$ points with stride one quarter window are
classified independently; on each window the admissible mechanism with the
lowest Bayesian information criterion,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BIC} = n\ln(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + 2\ln n,
  \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
wins ($n$: window size; RSS: regression residual sum of squares; two free
parameters per candidate). Consecutive windows with the same winner are merged
into a \emph{regime}, and the regression is refit over the merged span to
produce the reported slope, intercept, $R^2$, BIC, and
$\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$. Windows where no candidate is admissible are
labeled \emph{unclassified} (e.g., transition regions where trap-assisted
mixtures dominate \cite{chowdhury2025mwscas}); they are retained in the map
and treated conservatively downstream. The procedure is summarized in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:regimes}.

\subsubsection{Outputs}
The regime map lists, per (branch, state, window): mechanism, $v_{\mathrm{lo}}$,
$v_{\mathrm{hi}}$, slope, intercept, $R^2$, BIC, $\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$,
point count, and a \texttt{stanford\_valid} flag that is true for the bulk
mechanisms (ohmic, PF) representable by the Stanford current law and false for
the interface/tunneling mechanisms (Schottky, FN). When the per-cycle ensemble
is supplied, every cycle is classified independently and the \emph{mechanism
stability}---the fraction of cycles sharing the segment's dominant
mechanism---is reported, providing a variability metric at the level of
transport physics rather than current magnitude.

% ---- Algorithm 1 ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Conduction-regime classification (Stage 2)}
\label{alg:regimes}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Representative butterfly cycle $\{(V_i, I_i)\}$ with sequence index;
  $t_{\mathrm{ox}}$; $T$; admissibility bracket
  $\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}\in[1,60]$}
\KwOut{Regime map $\mathcal{R}$: \{branch, state, $[v_{\mathrm{lo}},
  v_{\mathrm{hi}}]$, mechanism, slope, $R^2$, BIC, $\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$,
  \texttt{stanford\_valid}\}}
split cycle into segments $\{$\textsc{set-hrs-pre}, \textsc{set-lrs-post},
  \textsc{reset-lrs-pre}, \textsc{reset-hrs-post}$\}$ at the sequence-ordered
  voltage extrema\;
\ForEach{segment $S$}{
  drop points with $|V|<\SI{0.05}{\V}$ or $|I|<\SI{e-13}{\A}$ or
    $|I|\ge 0.95\max_S|I|$ (compliance plateau)\;
  $w\leftarrow\max(8,\lceil |S|/3\rceil)$;\quad slide windows with stride
    $\lfloor w/4\rfloor$\;
  \ForEach{window $W$}{
    \ForEach{mechanism $M\in\{$ohmic, PF, Schottky, FN$\}$}{
      regress the linearization of $M$, Eqs.~(\ref{eq:ohmic})--(\ref{eq:fn}), on $W$\;
      reject $M$ if its slope-sign/range constraint fails\;
      \If{$M\in\{$PF, Schottky$\}$}{
        compute $\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ by Eq.~(\ref{eq:epsdyn}); reject $M$ if
        outside $[1,60]$\;}
    }
    label $W$ with the admissible $M$ of minimum BIC, Eq.~(\ref{eq:bic}), else
      \emph{unclassified}\;
  }
  merge consecutive same-label windows; refit each merged span; append to
    $\mathcal{R}$ with \texttt{stanford\_valid} $\leftarrow$
    $[M\in\{$ohmic, PF$\}]$\;
}
\If{per-cycle ensemble provided}{
  classify each cycle's segments as above (single window per segment); report
  per-state mechanism stability fractions\;}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Compact Model and Forward Simulation}
\label{sec:model}

We fit the Stanford--PKU RRAM model \cite{guan2012spice,jiang2014stanford}
(Verilog-A, \texttt{rram\_v\_1\_0\_0}) in which a conductive-filament gap $g$
is the state variable. The current law is the bulk hopping/tunneling
expression
\begin{equation}
  I = I_0\, e^{-g/g_0}\, \sinh\!\left(\frac{V_{\mathrm{tb}}}{V_0}\right),
  \label{eq:stanford_i}
\end{equation}
the gap evolves as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dg}{dt} = -\,\nu_0\,
  e^{-\frac{qE_a}{kT_{\mathrm{cur}}}}\,
  \sinh\!\left(\frac{\gamma\, a_0}{t_{\mathrm{ox}}}\,
  \frac{qV_{\mathrm{tb}}}{kT_{\mathrm{cur}}}\right),
  \qquad
  \gamma = \gamma_0 - \beta\, \frac{g^{3}}{g_0^{3}},
  \label{eq:stanford_gap}
\end{equation}
with local temperature $T_{\mathrm{cur}} = T_0 + |V_{\mathrm{tb}}
I\,R_{\mathrm{th}}|$ and $g$ clamped to $[g_{\min}, g_{\max}]$. Here $\nu_0$
($\equiv$ \texttt{Vel0}) is the gap velocity prefactor, $E_a$ the migration
barrier, $\gamma$ the local-field enhancement factor, $a_0$ the hopping
distance, and $F_{\min}$ the minimum switching field below which gap dynamics
are suppressed. A series resistance $R_s$ (probe/parasitic and soft-compliance
proxy) is placed in series with the device.

Three implementation details matter for reproducibility:

\subsubsection{Branch-split simulation}
The measured devices are asymmetric ($|V_{\mathrm{set}}|\neq
|V_{\mathrm{reset}}|$), while the Verilog-A model is largely symmetric; in
particular the released HSPICE model overrides $\gamma_0$ under negative bias.
We therefore simulate SET and RESET as separate transients with
polarity-split parameters: $V_0$, $\gamma_0$, $E_a$, $F_{\min}$, $\nu_0$,
$g_{\max}$, and the initial gap $g_{\mathrm{ini}}$ each carry independent
\_set/\_res values (SET starts in HRS, $g_{\mathrm{ini,set}}\!\approx\!
\SI{1.4}{\nano\meter}$; RESET starts in LRS,
$g_{\mathrm{ini,res}}=g_{\min}$), while $I_0$, $g_0$, $\beta$,
$R_{\mathrm{th}}$, $g_{\min}$, $t_{\mathrm{ox}}$, and $R_s$ are shared.
This yields a 21-parameter prior vector of which 16 are optimized by default.

\subsubsection{Quasi-static transient mapping}
Each measured branch is replayed as an HSPICE piecewise-linear (PWL)
transient whose time stamps follow the measured voltage samples at a nominal
rate of \SI{e5}{\volt\per\second}; the fitted $\nu_0$ and $E_a$ absorb the
effective time-scale, since a literal \SI{0.1}{\volt\per\second} sweep is
infeasible under the model's internal \texttt{\$bound\_step}. Simulated
current is interpolated back onto the measured time stamps, making the
comparison robust to HSPICE adaptive stepping, and the source-current sign is
flipped to the device convention.

\subsubsection{Compliance}
The SMU compliance is not part of the Verilog-A model; simulated currents are
clamped to $I_{\mathrm{cc}}=\SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere}$ \emph{after} simulation,
mirroring how the instrument truncates the measured SET branch.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 3: Regime-Conditioned Physics Priors}
\label{sec:priors}

Every model parameter $\theta_j$ receives a soft Gaussian prior
$(\mu_j,\sigma_j)$ and hard physical bounds $[l_j,u_j]$
(Table~\ref{tab:priors}). Structural constants ($t_{\mathrm{ox}}$, $a_0$,
$T_0$, $\varepsilon_r$) come from fabrication knowledge; the data-driven
priors are extracted from the representative curve \emph{inside the windows
designated by the regime map}, which is the central difference from
conventional prior extraction on fixed voltage ranges:

\subsubsection{LRS sinh prior ($I_0$, $V_0$)}
On the ohmic-classified window of \textsc{set-lrs-post} (fallback
$[0.02,0.6]\,$V if no map is available), excluding the compliance plateau
($|I|\ge0.95\,I_{\max}$), we fit $I = A\sinh(V/V_0)$ in log-parameter space by
Nelder--Mead. Since the LRS gap is pinned at $g_{\min}$,
$A = I_0 e^{-g_{\min}/g_0}$, giving
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{I_0} = A\, e^{g_{\min}/g_0^{\mathrm{prior}}},
  \label{eq:i0prior}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{I_0}$ propagated from the regression residual and the $g_0$
prior width.

\subsubsection{PF priors ($\gamma_0$, $E_a$), per branch}
On the PF-classified window of the HRS segment of each branch
(\textsc{set-hrs-pre} for SET; \textsc{reset-hrs-post} for RESET), restricted
to the correct half-sweep, the PF regression Eq.~(\ref{eq:pf}) yields slope
$\kappa$ and intercept $b$, which map to the model parameters as
\begin{align}
  \mu_{\gamma_0} &= 2\kappa\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon\, t_{\mathrm{ox}}}\;
    \frac{kT}{q}\;\frac{t_{\mathrm{ox}}}{a_0^{2}},
  &
  \mu_{E_a} &= -\,b\,\frac{kT}{q},
  \label{eq:pfmap}
\end{align}
clamped to the physical brackets of Table~\ref{tab:priors}
($\varepsilon=\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r$).

\emph{Honest fallback.} If the regime map shows \emph{no} PF window for a
branch---which our data show is the rule rather than the exception for
oxygen-rich devices whose HRS is ohmic$\to$FN \cite{chowdhury2025mwscas}---no
PF regression is forced onto non-PF data. Instead $\gamma_0$ and $E_a$ keep
literature-default means with doubled prior widths
($\sigma_{\gamma_0}\!=\!12$, $\sigma_{E_a}\!=\!0.4$), and a
\texttt{pf\_prior\_available=false} flag propagates to the identifiability
report. This single rule removes a silent failure mode of fixed-window prior
extraction and is itself a finding: the set of parameters with data-derived
priors becomes stoichiometry-dependent.

\subsubsection{Remaining priors}
$R_s$ is seeded from the measured LRS resistance (clamped to
$[10,10^{5}]\,\Omega$) or, failing that, sized so $V_{\max}/R_s\approx
I_{\mathrm{cc}}$; $g_0$, $\beta$, $R_{\mathrm{th}}$, $F_{\min}$, $\nu_0$, and
the gap geometry use literature-centered priors with generous widths
(Table~\ref{tab:priors}).

\subsection{Stage 4: Mechanism-Aware Objective Function}
\label{sec:loss}

Let $r_i$ denote the variance-scaled log-current residual at measured point
$i$ of branch $b\in\{\mathrm{SET},\mathrm{RESET}\}$:
\begin{equation}
  r_i = \frac{\log_{10}|I_i^{\mathrm{sim}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})| -
              \log_{10}|I_i^{\mathrm{meas}}|}{\sigma_i^{\log I}},
  \qquad
  \sigma_i^{\log I} = \max\!\left(
  \frac{\mathrm{IQR}_i}{1.35\,|I_i^{\mathrm{med}}|\ln 10},\, 0.05\right),
  \label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{IQR}_i$ is the interquartile current range across cycles at
sweep position $i$, i.e., the measured C2C spread acts as the per-point noise
model (Gaussian-equivalent via the 1.35 IQR factor), floored at 0.05 decades.

\emph{Mechanism-aware weighting.} Each point receives a weight $w_i$ from the
regime map: $w_i=1$ if the point lies in a window whose dominant mechanism is
representable by the Stanford current law (ohmic, PF) or in no classified
window; $w_i=w_{\mathrm{nb}}=0.3$ if it is covered \emph{only} by Schottky or
FN windows. The state (forward/return half-sweep) of each point is resolved
by its sequence position so that overlapping voltages map to the correct
regime. The branch losses are weighted means,
$L_b = \sum_i w_i r_i^{2} / \sum_i w_i$. This keeps the optimizer from
spending its error budget reproducing transport physics the model cannot
express, while \emph{still reporting} unweighted residuals in validation so
the mismatch outside the model's domain is visible, not hidden. Setting
$w_{\mathrm{nb}}=1$ exactly recovers the unweighted objective (used in the
ablation, Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

Two soft regularizers complete the objective:
\begin{equation}
  L(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = L_{\mathrm{SET}} + L_{\mathrm{RESET}}
  + \tfrac12 L_V + \tfrac1{10} L_{\mathrm{prior}},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $L_V = [(\hat V_{\mathrm{set}}-V_{\mathrm{set}})^2 +
(\hat V_{\mathrm{reset}}-V_{\mathrm{reset}})^2]/\sigma_V^2$ penalizes
misplaced switching thresholds ($\hat V$: voltage of extremal
$d\log_{10}|I|/dV$ in simulation; $\sigma_V$: median measured voltage step)
and $L_{\mathrm{prior}} = \sum_j (\theta_j-\mu_j)^2/\sigma_j^2$ is the
Gaussian prior penalty. Simulations returning non-finite currents receive
$L=10^{6}$. The weights $(1, 1, 0.5, 0.1)$ make the fit dominated by the
variance-scaled current match, with the priors acting as gentle guidance
rather than constraint.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 5: Global Bayesian Optimization and Local Refinement}
\label{sec:bo}

The active subset $\mathcal{A}$ (16 parameters by default: $I_0$, $g_0$,
$R_s$, and the polarity-split $V_0$, $\gamma_0$, $E_a$, $F_{\min}$, $\nu_0$,
$g_{\max}$, $g_{\mathrm{ini}}$) is optimized by Gaussian-process Bayesian
optimization (MATLAB \texttt{bayesopt}) with the expected-improvement-plus
acquisition function, 48 quasi-random seed evaluations followed by 160
adaptive iterations, and a deterministic objective (fixed RNG seed~0 for
reproducibility). Scale-type parameters ($I_0$, $g_0$, $R_s$,
$R_{\mathrm{th}}$, $\nu_0$, $F_{\min}$) are searched in log space over their
full physical bounds; structural parameters are searched over
$[\mu_j-3\sigma_j,\,\mu_j+3\sigma_j]$ clipped to $[l_j,u_j]$. Each objective
evaluation runs two HSPICE transients (one per branch), so a full fit costs
$2\times208$ simulator calls.

The BO optimum is then polished by Nelder--Mead (\texttt{fminsearch}, 100
evaluations) on the active subspace in log-parameter coordinates, which keeps
all positive physical parameters positive and rejects out-of-bound proposals
with a large penalty. BO supplies the global search; the simplex removes the
residual GP-surrogate bias near the optimum.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 6: Operational Identifiability}
\label{sec:ident}

Identifiability is evaluated \emph{at the fitted optimum}
$\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ (the Cram\'er--Rao bound is a property of the
estimate, not of the prior mean) and reported in two complementary forms.

\subsubsection{Fisher information / Cram\'er--Rao}
The scaled sensitivity matrix is built by central finite differences in
log-parameter space with relative step $10^{-3}$:
\begin{equation}
  S_{ij} = \frac{\partial \log_{10}|I_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})|}
                {\partial \ln\theta_j}\bigg|_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}},
  \qquad
  \widetilde S_{ij} = S_{ij}/\sigma_i^{\log I},
\end{equation}
over all SET and RESET points, giving
$F = \widetilde S^{\mathsf T}\widetilde S$. The effective standard deviation
of each parameter is $\sigma_j^{\mathrm{eff}} = \sqrt{[F^{+}]_{jj}}$
(pseudo-inverse, since $F$ is rank-deficient by construction when parameters
are non-identifiable), and the relative uncertainty
$\rho_j=\sigma_j^{\mathrm{eff}}/|\hat\theta_j|$ classifies each parameter:
\emph{identifiable} ($\rho_j<0.5$), \emph{weakly identifiable}
($0.5\le\rho_j<2$), \emph{non-identifiable} ($\rho_j\ge2$). The eigenspectrum
of $F$ is also reported; near-null eigenvectors expose \emph{sloppy}
parameter combinations (e.g., $I_0 e^{g/g_0}$ compensation) that pairwise
statistics miss.

\subsubsection{Profile likelihood}
Fisher analysis is local and linearized; it can both overstate and understate
practical identifiability \cite{raue2009}. We therefore compute
one-dimensional profile scans: for each active parameter, the loss
Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}) is re-evaluated at seven points spanning
$[\max(l_j,\hat\theta_j-2\sigma_j),\,\min(u_j,\hat\theta_j+2\sigma_j)]$ with
all other parameters held at $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$. Using
$\chi^2$-style thresholds on the loss rise at the window edges,
$\Delta=\min(\Delta_{\mathrm{lo}},\Delta_{\mathrm{hi}})$: $\Delta<1$ flags the
profile as \emph{flat} (practically non-identifiable), $1\le\Delta<4$ as weak,
$\Delta\ge4$ as identifiable. Flat directions found here are direct,
assumption-free evidence that DC data cannot constrain the parameter.

\subsubsection{Reporting and refitting policy}
Both verdicts are merged into a per-device \emph{identifiability report}
(parameter, $\hat\theta_j$, $\rho_j$, Fisher class, profile verdict,
PF-prior availability). Parameters classified non-identifiable are
\emph{reported at their prior values with unbounded uncertainty}---they are
explicitly not claimed as extracted. Optionally, a second fitting pass freezes
them at the prior mean and re-optimizes the identifiable subset
(\texttt{auto\_active} mode), which removes sloppy-direction wandering from
the point estimate. Because the regime map controls which priors are
data-driven, identifiability becomes mechanism-resolved: a device whose HRS
shows no PF window is \emph{expected} to lose $\gamma_0$/$E_a$
identifiability, turning the analysis into a falsifiable physical statement
linking deposition stoichiometry to extractable parameters.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stage 7: Validation and Cycle-to-Cycle Uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

\subsubsection{Residual diagnostics}
For the final $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ we report per-branch RMSE and MAE in
$\log_{10}$ decades (both weighted and unweighted, so the error inside and
outside the model's validity domain is distinguishable) and the
Durbin--Watson statistic of the residual sequence; DW values far from~2
indicate structured residuals, i.e., systematic model mismatch rather than
noise.

\subsubsection{Cluster bootstrap}
C2C variability is propagated to parameter uncertainty by a cluster bootstrap
over whole cycles: from the per-cycle ensemble of the fitted device, $B=120$
resamples draw cycles with replacement; each resample is collapsed to a
median curve by (branch, sweep position)---preserving the hysteresis
structure---and refit with a reduced BO budget ($8+24$ evaluations, seeded
from the same priors). The 2.5\% and 97.5\% quantiles of the resulting
parameter draws give 95\% confidence intervals, and the coefficient of
variation per parameter and condition quantifies which parameters are stable
device properties versus cycle-dependent quantities. Resampling whole cycles
(rather than points) respects the strong intra-cycle correlation of $I$--$V$
data; pointwise bootstrap would underestimate the intervals.

\subsubsection{Leave-one-cycle-out}
Optionally, each cycle is held out in turn, the model is refit to the
remainder, and the held-out RMSE distribution is reported, verifying that the
extracted parameter set generalizes across cycles rather than memorizing the
medoid.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Ablation Design}
\label{sec:ablation}

To isolate the contribution of each ingredient, every condition is fitted
four ways under identical model, data, and evaluation budget:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{physics\_regime\_bo} (proposed): regime-conditioned priors +
        mechanism-aware weighting + BO;
  \item \textbf{physics\_bo}: physics priors on fixed legacy windows,
        no regime information ($w_{\mathrm{nb}}=1$);
  \item \textbf{plain\_bo}: uninformative priors ($\sigma_j\to\infty$,
        search over full physical bounds) + BO --- the black-box baseline;
  \item \textbf{physics\_nobo}: regime-conditioned prior means without any
        optimization --- the un-tuned physics initial guess.
\end{enumerate}
Comparing (1) vs.\ (2) isolates the regime guidance; (1) vs.\ (3) the physics
priors; (1) vs.\ (4) the optimizer. All variants are scored with the
\emph{unweighted} validation metrics to avoid favoring the weighted-loss
variants by construction.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Implementation, Reproducibility, and Complexity}
\label{sec:impl}

Stages 0--2 are implemented in Python~3 (NumPy/pandas/Matplotlib); stages
3--7 in MATLAB driving Synopsys HSPICE (W-2024.09) through generated PWL
netlists referencing the Stanford \texttt{rram\_v\_1\_0\_0} Verilog-A source.
All stages are configuration-driven (YAML) with fixed RNG seeds; each fit
archives its priors, BO trace, refined optimum, Fisher matrix, profile scans,
identifiability report, and validation residuals. The dominant cost is HSPICE:
a point-estimate fit is $2\times(48+160+100)$ transients; profile likelihood
adds $2\times7\times|\mathcal{A}|$; the bootstrap adds
$2\times120\times32$ per condition. Wall-clock is a few hours per condition
on a single workstation; all conditions are embarrassingly parallel.

% ---- Algorithm 2 ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Physics-guided, identifiability-aware extraction (Stages 3--7)}
\label{alg:fit}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Representative curve $D$; per-cycle ensemble $\mathcal{E}$; regime map
  $\mathcal{R}$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:regimes}); config (bounds, budgets, seeds)}
\KwOut{$\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$; identifiability report; 95\% CIs;
  validation diagnostics}
\textbf{Priors:} fit LRS sinh on the ohmic window of $\mathcal{R}$
  $\rightarrow (\mu_{I_0},\mu_{V_0})$; per branch, fit PF regression on the
  PF window of $\mathcal{R}$ $\rightarrow (\mu_{\gamma_0},\mu_{E_a})$;
  \textbf{if} no PF window \textbf{then} keep defaults, double $\sigma$, flag
  \texttt{pf\_available=false}\;
assemble $(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{l},\mathbf{u})$ for
  all 21 parameters (Table~\ref{tab:priors}); compute point weights
  $w_i\in\{1, w_{\mathrm{nb}}\}$ from $\mathcal{R}$\;
\textbf{Global search:} $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathrm{BO}} \leftarrow$
  \texttt{bayesopt}$(L,\mathcal{A}$, EI-plus, $48+160$ evals, log transforms
  for scale params$)$ with $L$ from Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}); each $L$-call $=$ two
  HSPICE PWL transients $+$ compliance clamp\;
\textbf{Refine:} $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \leftarrow$ Nelder--Mead on
  $\ln\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathcal{A}}$ from $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathrm{BO}}$
  (100 evals, bound-rejecting)\;
\textbf{Fisher at optimum:} central differences ($10^{-3}$ rel.\ step)
  $\rightarrow F$; $\rho_j \leftarrow \sqrt{[F^{+}]_{jj}}/|\hat\theta_j|$;
  classify by $\rho_j < 0.5 / 2.0$\;
\textbf{Profile likelihood:} \ForEach{$\theta_j\in\mathcal{A}$}{
  scan 7 points on $[\hat\theta_j\pm2\sigma_j]\cap[l_j,u_j]$, others fixed;
  $\Delta\leftarrow$ min edge loss-rise; verdict by $\Delta<1/4$\;}
write identifiability report (Fisher class $+$ profile verdict $+$
  \texttt{pf\_available}); \textbf{optional:} freeze non-identifiable
  parameters at $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and re-run global search on the rest\;
\textbf{Uncertainty:} \For{$b=1$ \KwTo $120$}{
  resample cycles of $\mathcal{E}$ with replacement; aggregate by (branch,
  sweep position); refit with reduced budget ($8+24$)\;}
CIs $\leftarrow$ bootstrap 2.5/97.5\% quantiles; optional LOCO held-out RMSE\;
\textbf{Validate:} weighted and unweighted RMSE/MAE (decades), Durbin--Watson
  per branch; overlay plots\;
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Design Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

We document the known limitations explicitly. (i) The Stanford current law
cannot represent Schottky or FN transport; the proposed method does not fix
this structural limit---it bounds its impact (weighting) and reports where it
binds (regime map, unweighted residuals). Extending the model with parallel
interface/tunneling branches is deliberately left to future work to preserve
SPICE compatibility of the extracted parameter set. (ii) PF/Schottky
discrimination relies on the $\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ bracket $[1,60]$;
the bracket is generous and every assignment ships with its
$\varepsilon_r^{\mathrm{dyn}}$ so the sensitivity of conclusions to the
bracket can be audited. (iii) The quasi-static transient mapping means
$\nu_0$ and $E_a$ absorb the effective sweep time-scale; their absolute values
should be compared across conditions, not across measurement protocols.
(iv) Profile likelihoods are one-dimensional and may miss higher-order sloppy
manifolds; the Fisher eigenspectrum partially covers this. (v) The released
HSPICE Verilog-A model overrides $\gamma_0$ under negative bias, so
$\gamma_{0,\mathrm{res}}$ acts through a different effective path than
$\gamma_{0,\mathrm{set}}$; the branch-split simulation absorbs, but does not
remove, this asymmetry. (vi) The medoid cycle anchors the point estimate to
one real sweep; bootstrap CIs, not the point estimate, carry the C2C
statement.
